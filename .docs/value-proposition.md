# Value proposition

> 2026-09-22. Brainstormed with Robert; the one-liner is ratified and sits in
> `.trail-boss/project.md` (`value_prop`). This is the reasoning behind it, so
> it does not get re-argued.

## The line

**Answers about the estate from evidence, not from asking someone.**

Census, Atlas and Move It are one thing seen at three altitudes. The value is
the thread that runs through them, not the sum of the features. Today the
governance reader gets answers from emails, a weekly meeting, and whoever
happens to remember. Each module replaces one kind of asking:

- **Census** replaces *what is on that box?* with what the collector saw last
  night.
- **Atlas** replaces *what does this system touch?* with observed connections
  a human has classified.
- **Move It** replaces *where is that feature?* with where the pings saw the
  build arrive.

## Why this line and not a features list

- **It names the enemy.** The competitor is not another tool, it is
  institutional memory. That is what makes the value obvious to the reader,
  who has been on the wrong end of it.
- **It carries the principles.** Observe → human-tag → view
  (`system-graph-principles.md`). Never infer from naming. Declared is drawn
  dashed. Went live unsigned gets a "!". Every one of those is the same
  promise: the page will not tell you something nobody saw.
- **It scales to modules not yet built.** Anything that turns a question you
  would have to ask into a question you can look up belongs. Anything else
  does not.

## Angles considered and set aside

- **"The department's ground truth."** Punchier, but overclaims. Move It's
  Team A positions are asserted, and the design is honest about that. *From
  evidence* leaves room for the dashed line.
- **"Governance without meetings."** Sells the outcome, but reads as a process
  pitch and invites the objection that meetings do other things too.

## As a header

*Census: what the estate is doing, from evidence.* The three module names sit
under it as the three questions it answers.
